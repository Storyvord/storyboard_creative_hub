# Loop 2 Review

## Verdict in one line
The restructure landed — Ch 8 is the right shape, Ch 0 names a filmmaker problem, and the artifact chain mostly holds — but the script is twice as long as the author thinks it is, the through-line set in Ch 0 is never paid off in language, and at least three chapter handoffs trust the editor to do work the narration should do.

## Is this shippable? (yes / yes-with-edits / no)
**Yes-with-edits**, but the edits are NOT all small. The total runtime — adding the chapter targets verbatim (55+75+70+65+80+85+70+70+55+25) — is 10 minutes 50 seconds, not the "5:30-6" the change-log claims. At the stated word counts and 140 WPM, the narration alone is ~10 minutes. The Linear-style playbook caps demos at 6 min. So one of two things has to happen in v3: either (a) the author re-does the runtime math and accepts this is a 10-min video, in which case some chapters need tightening to bring it down to 7-8 min, OR (b) the original "5:30" target was real and three or four chapters need to lose 40-60% of their words. The rest of the issues — outro antecedent broken by the new cold open, Ch 3 ending on silence, Ch 4 visual carrying a load the narration should share, the through-line orphaned after Ch 0 — are all polish-pass fixes. Length is the structural one.

## NEW issues caught this round (must be things not in Loop 1 review)

1. **Runtime math is wrong by ~5 minutes.** Change-log claims "v2 dropped to ~5:30-6." Stated chapter runtimes sum to 10:50. Stated word counts (1,402) at 140 WPM are 10:00 of pure VO. The script is the same length as v1 — possibly longer — but the author thinks it shrank. Either the targets need recutting or the perception is wrong; v3 author needs to be told the truth before they polish a script they think is half its actual size.

2. **"Tired of the old way" no longer has an antecedent.** Ch 9 outro retains *"Built with the directors, producers, and crew who were tired of the old way."* In v1 the "old way" was the chaotic desk in Ch 0 — coffee-stained PDF, sticky notes, "references final final." The Ch 0 rewrite replaced that with three thoughtful prep rooms (Pinterest board, marked-up script, budget folder). Those rooms aren't "the old way" — they're competent prep. The contrast the outro depends on no longer exists in the video. Either rewrite the outro line or restore one chaos-beat in Ch 0.

3. **The through-line is set in the brief, named in Ch 0, and never spoken again.** The stated through-line is *"Three people are holding three different films in their heads… The whole video is the act of dragging the three heads into the same frame."* Ch 0 narration delivers it once ("The director sees one film. The DP sees another. The producer's holding a budget for a third."). After that the script never returns to the three-heads idea in language. Ch 8's climax should *be* the payoff ("now they're holding the same film") but the narration there is about portrait promotion, not about three heads converging. The thesis is orphaned.

4. **Ch 3 ends on a visual with no narration over it.** Last narrated line is *"Tag a character with an `@`. The next image knows who's standing there."* The chapter then shows a drag-reorder visual with no voice. v1 had a closer ("Coverage, in the order you'd shoot it. Ready to be seen") — Loop 1 correctly cut "Ready to be seen" but the v2 author cut the whole line and didn't replace it. The chapter stops mid-thought and hands an empty mic to Ch 4. The drag-reorder beat needs one line on top of it.

5. **Ch 4's per-scene style override has the same retroactive-render risk Ch 8 was fixed for.** v2 fixed Ch 8 to clarify that previously rendered shots don't re-render when a portrait is promoted. But Ch 4 visual says *"switches to 'Sketch.' That row regenerates in pencil."* Per the catalog, changing `storyboarding_type` flows into the next generation — it doesn't auto-regenerate the existing shots in that row. The visual implies a magic re-render of an already-populated filmstrip. Either show the user re-running bulk previz on that scene (a click, a moment), or rephrase to *"The next time those shots draw, they draw in pencil."*

6. **"The cost shows before you spend it" reads broken aloud.** "Shows" is intransitive here and the line wants an object. When spoken, it sounds like a fragment. A working filmmaker would parse it as "the cost shows… before you spend… it?" — the "it" lands on nothing. Rewrite below.

7. **Two consecutive chapter openings are silent visuals that depend on the editor.** Ch 4 *ends* on a visual cue ("every face in it is a stranger") that exists only in the [VISUAL] block — never spoken. Ch 5 *opens* on "a close-up of 'Alex,' generic and not-yet-Alex" — also only in the [VISUAL] block. Two consecutive unspoken setups put a lot of weight on the editor delivering generic placeholder faces AND on the viewer noticing the genericness twice. One of these needs a spoken cue.

8. **Ch 7 → Ch 8 has a phantom artifact handoff.** Ch 7 hands "a single rendered frame in the history strip" to Ch 8. Ch 8 opens with cursor on "the character's previz history strip on the Character Detail page." Those are two different history strips on two different pages. The cut from Creative Space to Character Detail is unmotivated — the artifact doesn't actually travel; the page just changes. The Loop 1 review explicitly praised the artifact-handoff discipline; this is a regression.

9. **The "/" in "scenes / faces / places" branding — Ch 0's "same faces, same places, every frame" — is now slightly miscalibrated.** v1 said "The same characters. The same locations. The same look." v2 says "The same faces. The same places. Every frame." The change is good (more filmmaker-y), but "faces / places" is a near-rhyme that draws attention to itself in a script otherwise allergic to rhyme. Read aloud, it sounds like a children's book line. Worth resmoothing.

10. **The slideshow climax is one click in the script, on an unspecified surface.** Ch 8 visual: *"The slideshow button is hit. Full-screen takeover."* The slideshow button lives in the storyboard toolbar (per the feature catalog) — but in Ch 8 we just jumped from Character Detail to "a wide grid of the whole storyboard." The pull-back is described, but the click on the slideshow button has no clear setup. v1's slideshow click was clearer because it lived inside Ch 4 where the storyboard was already on screen. Now it requires a navigation jump the visual cue under-explains.

## Author's two open concerns — adjudicated

1. **Ch 8 austerity — is it too austere?** **Yes, add one line.** At 70 words / 55s and only two narrated sentences before going silent, the climax doesn't have enough narration carrying intent — it has setup ("every image remembers what came before it") and demonstration ("promote an older take") and then the slideshow just *happens*. The viewer needs one line that names what the slideshow IS so the silence afterward is meaningful, not confusing. This is also where the orphaned through-line should be paid off.

   **Exact line to add, dropped right before the slideshow button is hit:** *"Three people. One film. Drawn."* — short, declarative, mirrors the Ch 0 setup, gives the silent climax a thesis to hang on. Place it as the line that fires as the cursor moves toward the slideshow button. Then voice out. Then 8+ seconds of silent shots playing.

2. **Ch 4 → Ch 5 implicit handoff — trust misplaced?** **Yes, add a spoken cue.** Two visual-only handoffs back-to-back (Ch 4's "every face is a stranger" closing visual, then Ch 5's "generic, not-yet-Alex" opening visual) is asking the editor to do narrative work that should be the narrator's job. Even with a perfect storyboard cut, the viewer might just register "OK, scrolling back up, more storyboard" instead of "wait, these faces are all wrong."

   **Exact line to add at the end of Ch 4, over the scroll-back-to-top visual:** *"The film is drawn. But the people in it are still strangers."* Then cut into Ch 5 on the click of @Alex. That single line does the entire handoff. (Bonus: it directly sets up "One face per character" as the answer.)

## Per-chapter notes (concise — only NEW or what v1 missed)

### Chapter 0
- The three-rooms cold open works. But: the line *"The director sees one film. The DP sees another. The producer's holding a budget for a third."* — "for a third" is a dangling preposition that lands on nothing when spoken. The third *what*? "A third film" was the implicit antecedent but it's three sentences back. Tighten: *"...a budget for a third one."*
- *"The same faces. The same places. Every frame."* — "faces / places" rhymes, which the rest of the script studiously avoids. Consider: *"Same faces. Same locations. Every frame."* — breaks the rhyme without losing the rhythm.
- "This is Creative Hub" lands at line ~5 — earlier than the 0:23 mark the director note targets. Either the visual gap before it needs to be longer, or the director note's timing prediction is off.

### Chapter 1
- Solid. The Loop 1 fixes landed cleanly.
- Minor: *"every slug, every action line, every cue, where it belongs"* — the four-comma pile reads as a list trailing off. Try em-dash before the last clause: *"every slug, every action line, every cue — where it belongs."*

### Chapter 2
- Solid. The "actual parsed count" instruction is a real production-ops win.
- Watch: the line "The view a first AD would build by hand on day one of prep. Already built." — beautiful, but it's a *thesis* line in a chapter that's otherwise procedural. It's actually doing some of the heavy lifting the through-line should be doing. Consider it the chapter's center of gravity.

### Chapter 3
- **New issue (see #4 above):** chapter ends with no closing narration. The drag-reorder visual goes mute. Add one line.
- Suggested closer over the drag-reorder visual: *"The order they'd be on the strip."* (Calls back the "shot list" line from earlier in the chapter and motivates Ch 4's "every shot, one page.")

### Chapter 4
- **New faithfulness issue (see #5 above):** the style-override row regenerating in pencil reads as auto-regeneration. Catalog says style flows into *next* generation; existing renders don't update without a re-run.
- **New handoff issue (see #7 above):** "every face in it is a stranger" is in the [VISUAL] block, not narration. Add the line per concern #2.
- The "filmstrip plays end-to-end" visual instruction is ambiguous — these are static shot cards, not video. Does the camera pan across them? Does the slideshow run? (No — slideshow is held for Ch 8.) Clarify the direction or cut "plays" — "One scene's filmstrip reads end-to-end" or "the camera tracks across one finished filmstrip."

### Chapter 5
- Strongest chapter. "What's bruised. What hasn't healed yet." is publish-ready.
- New issue: the opening visual depends on the editor finding "the same Alex shot we left Ch 4 on." That requires continuity of frame across the chapter boundary. The visual direction should be explicit — name the specific shot. Suggest: *"Hold on the storyboard shot from the alley sequence — Alex in WS, generic placeholder face."*
- "Generate a portrait. Or upload a real one — a casting photo, a screen test. Either way, that becomes her face." — "her" assumes Alex is a woman. The catalog doesn't gender Alex. Probably fine in context (the wardrobe note about the cut and the diner/hospital appearances build a coherent character), but worth flagging.

### Chapter 6
- *"Open one, and the location opens up"* — "open / opens up" is an echo. Read aloud, it sounds like a tongue-twister. Replace.
- The director's note about cropping above the Demo Data bands is doing real work — keep that note loud. A camera op who skims the script will need it bold.

### Chapter 7
- **New issue (see #6 above):** *"The cost shows before you spend it."* — "shows" needs an object or a different verb. Spoken aloud the line is broken.
- **New issue (see #8 above):** the artifact handoff to Ch 8 is between two different history strips on two different pages. Visual cue needs to bridge them — maybe the cursor drags the new frame *to* the character row, or there's a connecting cut.

### Chapter 8

## Read-aloud flags (lines that don't flow when spoken)

- **"The director sees one film. The DP sees another. The producer's holding a budget for a third."** — "for a third" dangles. Replacement: *"The director sees one film. The DP sees another. The producer's holding the budget for a third one. And nobody's drawn it yet."*

- **"Every slug, every action line, every cue, where it belongs."** — four-comma trail. Replacement: *"Every slug, every action line, every cue — where it belongs."*

- **"The same faces. The same places. Every frame."** — internal rhyme out of register. Replacement: *"Same faces. Same locations. Every frame."*

- **"Set the frame. Set the model. The cost shows before you spend it."** — "shows" is broken. Replacement: *"Set the frame. Set the model. You see the cost before you spend it."* Or: *"...The price is on screen before you spend it."*

- **"Open one, and the location opens up"** (Ch 6) — open/opens echo. Replacement: *"Open one, and the binder fills in — the establishing image, every variation you've tried, every scene that shoots there."*

- **"Promote an older take. The next frame the film draws picks her up."** (Ch 8) — "picks her up" is colloquial in a way that reads as different register from the rest of the chapter. Possible replacement: *"Promote an older take. The next frame draws to her."* (Lets the line stay short and on-tone.)

## Visual/VO sync issues

- **Ch 1, ⌘1/⌘2/⌘3 keyboard overlay:** narration says *"Rewrite a line, cut a scene, rename a character. The breakdown follows."* The shortcut overlay shows scene heading / action / character commands — but the narration is about *editing*, not *navigation*. The visual is doing one thing (keyboard nav), the voice is doing another (consequences of edits). Either drop the keyboard overlay here or move it to after the "Lock the script" beat.

- **Ch 4, bulk previz cards filling top-to-bottom:** narration says *"Pick the scenes. Let it run."* The visual then shows a tight frame on one card, then pulls out. But "Let it run" is the instruction; what plays under it is several seconds of shimmer with no voice. That's fine, but the next line — *"A flashback needs a different look"* — has to land right as the per-scene dropdown opens. The pacing isn't specified; the director note should mandate the timing.

- **Ch 5, Scene Look Editor:** narration *"Scene by scene, decide what she's wearing. What's bruised. What hasn't healed yet."* lands during the drawer rise. But the drawer animation is one continuous motion, and the line is three beats. Either the line needs to land *after* the drawer is fully open (so each beat reads against a settled UI), or the drawer needs to pause at each beat. Currently ambiguous.

- **Ch 8, the click-to-promote → next regen → wide grid sequence:** that's three distinct visual beats over two short narration sentences. The middle beat (the single shot regenerating) has no voice over it. Risk: the viewer doesn't register that what they're seeing is *one* shot updating, not all of them. Either narrate that middle beat, or hold longer on the single card so it reads.

## Faithfulness re-audit (v2 only — anything new?)

- **Ch 4 per-scene style override row "regenerates in pencil"** — see issue #5. Per catalog, style flows into the next generation; existing renders don't auto-update. Either the user clicks a re-run, or the language needs to soften to "the next time it draws, it draws in pencil." Cut from style change → re-run click → row redrawing reads honest.

- **Ch 5 "Cut above left eye, day-old"** — the catalog's Scene Look Editor accepts free-text Continuity Notes that feed the generation. Accurate. But the result must visibly show the cut. If the rendered image doesn't reliably include the cut on this take, this beat is fragile. Director should be told: shoot until you get a render that obviously shows the wound.

- **Ch 5 wardrobe drag-in** — catalog confirms wardrobe items are a category-tabbed picker, not a freeform drag. The visual *"A wardrobe item is dragged in"* should specify whether this is a click-to-add from the rack (which is the actual UI) or implies drag-and-drop. Minor but the spec should be explicit so the editor doesn't fake a drag the product doesn't do.

- **Ch 6 reference chip → hero regenerates** — accurate per catalog, with the same caveat as the Loop 1 review: shoot this with a multi-image-conditioning model (e.g. `gpt-image-2`) or the visual undercuts the claim.

- **Ch 7 "credits-per-image" shown on screen** — accurate per catalog. Keep.

- **Ch 8 promote-portrait → next regen** — fixed correctly. The director note about not implying mass re-render is the right kind of paranoid.

No major new fabrications. The one new risk is Ch 4's implied style auto-regeneration.

## Final direction for v3

This is the last pass. The v3 author should resist any urge to restructure further. The shape of v2 is right — Ch 8 as silent climax, Ch 0 as filmmaker pain, Ch 5 protected and lyrical. v3 is polish + one structural decision.

**What to fix (exactly):**

1. **The structural one — runtime math.** Confront the 10:50 number. Either accept it and tighten Chs 1, 2, 5, 7 each by ~20 seconds (a sentence cut from each), or recommit to the 5:30 target and rewrite at half the current word count. Don't ship a script that thinks it's 6 minutes but plays at 11.

2. **Add the through-line payoff to Ch 8.** Insert *"Three people. One film. Drawn."* as the last narrated line before the slideshow. This pays off Ch 0 and gives the silent climax a thesis.

3. **Add the Ch 4 → Ch 5 spoken handoff.** *"The film is drawn. But the people in it are still strangers."* — over the scroll-back-to-top visual, ending Ch 4.

4. **Add a closer to Ch 3.** *"The order they'd be on the strip."* — over the drag-reorder visual.

5. **Fix the outro antecedent.** Either restore one chaos-beat to Ch 0 (a discarded shot list, a dead-end recce photo) so "the old way" lands, OR rewrite the outro line to be self-contained: *"Built with the directors, producers, and crew who do this work."* (Less punchy, but honest now.)

6. **Read-aloud fixes** from the section above — six lines total, all small.

**What NOT to touch (because it's working):**

- **Ch 2's "The view a first AD would build by hand on day one of prep. Already built."** Untouchable.
- **Ch 9's "Open it. Start with one scene."** Untouchable.

**What to add if anything is missing:**

- One spoken through-line callback somewhere in the middle of the video — Ch 5 or Ch 7 — that reminds the viewer of the three-heads-one-frame thesis. Optional but it would close the thematic loop.
- A music cue spec for Ch 8. The silent slideshow climax depends on music carrying the emotional load. The script currently says "Music up. Voice out." Spec the music *type* — score, not needle-drop. The whole video has been quiet.
