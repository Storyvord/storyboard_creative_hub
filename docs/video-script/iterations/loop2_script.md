# Creative Hub — Demo Video Script (v2)

**Through-line:** Three people are holding three different films in their heads, and nobody's drawn one of them yet. A screenplay walks into the workspace. A shot-ready film walks out — same cast, same locations, same look, page one to the last frame. The whole video is the act of dragging the three heads into the same frame.

---

## Chapter 0 — Introduction / Overview
**Runtime target:** ~55s  **Word count:** 122
**Visual opening:** Half of a phone call we can only hear in fragments — a director's voice on speaker, saying "no, the flashback should feel pencil, not film." Cut to a printed script on a desk with a DP's hand-drawn frame in the margin. Cut to a Pinterest board open on a second monitor. Cut to a third hand pulling a budget out of a folder. Three different rooms, three different prep moments. Cut to black.
**Artifact carried in from previous chapter:** None.
**Artifact handed to next chapter:** The screenplay itself — a PDF dragged toward the screen.

### Narration
> Pre-production is where the film is actually made.
>
> Not on set.
>
> [VISUAL: Two beats of black, then the three rooms intercut once more — the marked-up script, the lookbook, the budget.]
>
> The director sees one film. The DP sees another. The producer's holding a budget for a third. And nobody's drawn it yet.
>
> [VISUAL: Cut to black. One beat.]
>
> This is Creative Hub.
>
> [VISUAL: Dark UI fades up. The sidebar slides in — Script, Scenes, Characters, Locations, Storyboard. Emerald accent.]

>

>

- First 8 seconds: no product, no logo, no brand name. The phone-call fragment carries the cold open.
- The three-rooms intercut is the *filmmaker* version of the pain — head-of-department misalignment, not spreadsheets. Cast these visuals with prep specificity: a Sharpie-annotated script, a real Pinterest tile of cinematography references, a budget worksheet on actual letterhead.
- Brand name "Creative Hub" lands at ~0:23. Hold off on "Storyvord."
- Hold the sidebar reveal long enough to read all five chapter labels.
- Music: in under the sidebar reveal. Out by the end of "Every frame."

## Chapter 4 — Storyboarding
**Runtime target:** ~80s  **Word count:** 175
**Visual opening:** Pull back from the single scene's shot row into the full Storyboard page — every scene of the film stacked as horizontal filmstrips, each one a row of shot cards waiting for images.
**Artifact carried in from previous chapter:** The scene's empty shot strip.
**Artifact handed to next chapter:** A finished filmstrip — one shot card holds on a generated image of Alex whose face we haven't yet locked.

### Narration
> Every scene. Every shot. One page.
>
> [VISUAL: Slow vertical scroll down the Storyboard. Each scene is a filmstrip. Shot cards mostly empty. Aspect ratio across the top reads 2.35:1.]
>
> Set the aspect ratio. Set the look — sketch, storyboard, HD, anime. The whole film draws in that language.
>
> [VISUAL: Style dropdown opens. Cursor lands on "Storyboard." Top-right, the Bulk Previz button activates.]
>
> Pick the scenes. Let it run.
>
> [VISUAL: Cards across multiple scenes flip to the Saving → Queued → Rendering shimmer. A wand icon rotates. Tiles fill in one by one, top to bottom. Frame tight on a single card to read the micro-state.]
>
> [VISUAL: Pull out as more tiles complete. One scene's filmstrip plays end-to-end — wide, medium, close, insert — the alley sequence, in cinematic 2.35.]
>
> A flashback needs a different look — pencil instead of color. Override the style on that scene alone. The rest of the film holds.
>
> [VISUAL: Per-scene style dropdown opens on a single filmstrip, switches to "Sketch." That row regenerates in pencil. The scenes above and below stay HD.]
>
> [VISUAL: Scroll back up to the top of the storyboard. The film is drawn — but every face in it is a stranger.]

### Director notes
- The Saving → Queued → Rendering shimmer is the hero visual of this chapter. Frame tight on one shot card so the micro-state reads.
- The per-scene style override is the killer detail. Show the dropdown opening, the row regenerating, the surrounding scenes staying untouched. Hold one beat after the row finishes.
- The chapter ends on a scroll back to the top — *not* on a slideshow. The slideshow is held back for Chapter 8 where it lands as climax.
- The "every face in it is a stranger" cue is implicit — the storyboard images are generic placeholders pre-character-lock. Let that emptiness motivate Chapter 5.

### Director notes
