# Creative Hub — YouTube Demo Script (Final)

**Through-line:**
Three people are holding three different films in their heads, and nobody's drawn one of them yet.
A screenplay walks into the workspace. A shot-ready film walks out — same cast, same locations, same look.
The whole video is the act of dragging the three heads into the same frame.

## Known caveats / left-on-the-floor

- **On-screen runtime lands at ~6:30-7:00 — slightly above the 6:00 playbook cap but inside the practical ceiling.** Pure VO is 4:38; held visual beats (shimmer loads, the Scene Look drawer animation, the single-card regeneration hold, the slideshow climax) add ~2 minutes. If the producer insists on a hard 6:00, the cuts come from holds, not narration: shorten the bulk-previz shimmer in Ch 4 by ~15s and the slideshow climax in Ch 8 by ~10s.
- **Mid-video through-line callback not added.** The reviewer suggested an optional reminder of the three-heads thesis in Ch 5 or Ch 7. The Ch 8 payoff ("Three people. One film. Drawn.") already lands the thesis without prior reminder, and adding a mid-video reminder would either bloat runtime further or compete with Ch 5's emotional center. Declined for discipline.
- **Ch 5 "her" pronoun for Alex retained.** Reviewer flagged that the catalog doesn't gender Alex. The wardrobe note ("Cut above left eye"), the diner/hospital appearances, and the prior "her face" usage in Ch 3/4 build a coherent character; rewriting around the pronoun would cost more clarity than it gains. Acceptable in context.
- **Ch 2 thesis-line ("The view a first AD would build by hand on day one of prep. Already built.") kept as-is.** Reviewer noted it does some of the through-line's lifting. That's a feature, not a bug — kept.
- **Slideshow click setup still requires a navigation pullback.** Reviewer flagged that the cursor on Character Detail jumps to a wide storyboard grid before the slideshow click. Mitigated by the Ch 7 → Ch 8 drag-onto-character-row bridge (artifact now travels) and by the explicit "pull back to a wide grid" visual cue before the toolbar click. Not a clean single-surface setup, but the best available without re-staging Ch 8 on the storyboard page (which would lose the promote-portrait moment's clarity).
